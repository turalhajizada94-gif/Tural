### 2. "No scholarship means no full-time PhD" is only true while you are international

This is the second thing worth correcting, and it is decisive.

Monash's *RTP Scholarship Procedure*, clause **2.12**: "**All domestic students at the University
will receive the RTP Fees Offset.**" Clause 2.9: the RTP Fees Offset "covers the graduate research
student's tuition fees that would have otherwise been payable by the student. The University fully
offsets the applicable tuition fee." Clause 2.11: up to 4 years EFT for doctoral candidates.

That is not competitive and not merit-ranked. It is automatic for every domestic student.

So the economics are completely different before and after PR:

| | International (now) | Domestic (after PR) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tuition, PhD in MNHS | ~$53,800/yr, indicative 2026 | **$0** — RTP Fees Offset, automatic |
| Living stipend | Needs competitive MGS/RTP award | Needs competitive MGS/RTP award |
| Can you enrol full-time without winning a stipend? | Practically no — you would be paying ~$188k over 3.5 years | **Yes** |

The scholarship you were told you cannot win is the **living allowance** ($37,145/yr, 2026 rate,
tax-free for full-time). Not being awarded it does not block a full-time PhD once you are domestic.
It means you fund your own living costs.

And that is more tractable than it sounds, because of how Monash defines study load in the *Graduate
Research Enrolment Procedure*, clause 1.7: full-time enrolment is "the equivalent of at least **four
days** each week"; part-time is at least two days. A full-time Monash PhD is a four-day-a-week
commitment. That leaves roughly a day a week for paid work while still being enrolled, ranked and
treated as a full-time candidate. The RTP Scholarship Procedure (4.28.2, 4.29) explicitly permits
paid employment alongside candidature.

## What your WAM actually blocks

Two different thresholds are being conflated.

**Admission — you meet it.** *Graduate Research Admissions Procedure* 1.8.1 sets the minimum for a
doctoral course as a four-year bachelor's degree including a research thesis or project with "a
minimum overall average grade of an honours degree equivalent to the **Second Class Honours Division
A**", i.e. H2A, i.e. 70–79. At 70–75 you are inside the admission requirement, not outside it.
Clause 1.8.3 also allows "a qualification, or combination of qualifications and relevant
professional experience, deemed equivalent". Your GDPA is an APAC-accredited fourth-year with a
genuine supervised research project (PSY4400–PSY4404), which is what clause 1.8.1 is asking for.

**Scholarship — this is where 80+ bites.** *RTP Scholarship Procedure* 3.3.1 says the Graduate
Research Committee will "prioritise applicants who hold an Australian or New Zealand bachelor's
degree with **first class honours** or qualifications and/or research experience **deemed equivalent
by the University**". Monash's own apply page is blunt about it: "Meeting the minimum academic entry
requirements for admission does not mean you will be considered competitive for a scholarship — the
bar is higher and the field is competitive."

So what admissions told you is a fair description of the central round. But note two things in the
wording. It is a *prioritisation*, not a hard cut-off. And "or qualifications and/or research
experience deemed equivalent" is an explicit alternative route to H1-equivalence.

Clause 3.2 says awards are made on "academic record, **research output** and **prior research
experience**" — three inputs, only one of which is your WAM. And the Admissions Procedure 1.10 lists
what is actually weighed:

- 1.10.1 — "the mark or grade and **extent of any research component** completed as part of a study
  program; thesis examiner reports; ... the extent to which units relate to the acquisition of
  research skills"
- 1.10.2 — "research outputs, e.g. books, journal articles, conference proceedings"
- 1.10.3 — "relevant professional, research or clinical experience"

That is the lever. A strong mark in the GDPA research project units, a publication or conference
abstract out of your thesis, and documented research experience are the things that move you from
"70–75 WAM, not competitive" toward "research experience deemed equivalent".

**Verify with MGRO:** whether scholarship ranking uses your overall GDPA WAM or weights the research
project units. This materially changes your strategy over the next six weeks and is worth a direct
written question.

## The funding routes that are not WAM-ranked

This is the part most applicants miss. The central MGS/RTP round is not the only way to get a
stipend, and the alternatives are selected by supervisors and project teams, not by a
university-wide academic ranking.

**1. Advertised, grant-funded project stipends.** Monash schools routinely advertise PhD stipends
attached to specific ARC or NHMRC grants — same $37,145/yr rate, 3.5 years, plus support funds. The
published application instructions for these are telling: you "Select **Admission Only** as the
Application Type" and then name the specific scholarship under "Research Program Funding". In other
words these sit *outside* the competitive rounds, have their own deadlines, and are awarded on fit
with the project. Watch Supervisor Connect and the MNHS graduate research pages continuously.

**2. Turning Point / MARC scholarships.** Turning Point runs an EOI-based PhD scholarship program
supported through MNHS's Graduate Research Program — full-time stipend, maximum 3.5 years, enrolment
via Eastern Health Clinical School. You email a completed EOI form to
`research-turningpoint@monash.edu` naming a program area. Their live projects include app-based and
neurocognitive addiction work. This is an EOI-and-interview process, not a WAM ladder, and it is
squarely in your area of interest.

**3. National Industry PhD Program.** Given the project you were drawn to was built with an industry
partner (Cogstate), this is worth knowing about. The Industry Linked stream pays a stipend top-up of
at least $10,000/yr from the industry partner plus $7,135/yr from the Commonwealth (2026 rates) for
up to four years full-time. Requirements: you must be eligible for the RTP Fees Offset (so:
domestic) and hold an RTP Stipend "or similar scholarship" — it is a top-up, not a base stipend.
Applications go through the university's Graduate Research Liaison Office, not from you directly.
There is also a part-time stream ($5,000 + $3,567/yr, up to eight years).

**4. Project top-up.** The listing you have quotes $6,000/yr for three years. Under the RTP
Scholarship Procedure a "top-up" is by definition "held in addition to a main stipend scholarship",
so it supplements rather than replaces a base award. Also, indexation does not apply to top-ups
(4.3).

**5. QIMR Berghofer.** If you want to follow this specific research line rather than stay at Monash:
QIMR requires PhD students to hold a living stipend and a tuition scholarship, and to enrol through
UQ, QUT, Griffith or Bond. They run a limited international PhD scholarship program (the most recent
EOI round closed 24 August 2026, with commencement required by 31 July 2027 — so watch for the next
round). Their normal route is: pick a project, email the supervisor with CV and transcripts, request
an interview. Contact `graduateeducation@qimrb.edu.au`. Note their admission process can take up to
12 weeks and runs in parallel with university admission.

## Part-time: what it does and does not cost you

You asked whether part-time carries the same weight. Honest answer, split into the parts that matter.

**What does not change.** The award is identical. A PhD is a PhD; the testamur does not record study
load, and examination standards are the same. Course duration is 3–4 years EFT full-time, 6–8 years
part-time. Part-time is explicitly available for the Clinical Neuropsychology doctorate, for
instance.

**What does change, and this is the real problem.** *RTP Scholarship Procedure* 3.3.2: the Committee
prioritises applicants who "**can enrol and undertake their research on a full-time basis**". So
electing part-time actively lowers your scholarship ranking. That creates a trap worth naming
plainly: you would be going part-time because you did not get a stipend, and going part-time is
itself a reason you would not get a stipend.

Other concrete costs:

- Part-time stipends at Monash are available only to a domestic recipient "with carer's
  responsibilities, a medical condition or a disability, which prevents them from studying
  full-time", case by case (4.42). General preference does not qualify.
- Full-time stipends are non-taxable; **part-time stipends are taxable** (4.20).
- Many funded projects require full-time enrolment as a condition of the award. The listing you have
  specifies "Time commitment: Full-time".
- Part-time enrolment "may not be available for some courses and cohorts" (*Enrolment Procedure*
  4.15).
- Practically: for a project involving trial recruitment, cohort data collection and an industry
  partner's development cycle, a two-day week is a poor fit. Research momentum, publication rate and
  team integration all suffer, and those are what determine whether a PhD leads anywhere.

**What is genuinely reassuring.** Part-time is not a one-way door. Students may transfer between
full-time and part-time via an attendance variation form (*Enrolment Procedure* 4.14), and holders of
part-time scholarships "may convert to full-time at any stage" (*RTP Procedure* 4.43).

**The conclusion I would draw.** Do not choose part-time as a workaround for missing a stipend. The
better version of that same trade-off is: enrol **full-time as a domestic student** with tuition
covered by the RTP Fees Offset, and use the fact that full-time means four days a week to hold
about one day a week of paid work. You keep full-time status, full scholarship eligibility for
later rounds, project eligibility and research momentum. Reserve part-time for a genuine
circumstance, not as a funding strategy.

## Family sponsorship of $37,145/yr — where it helps and where it does not

Short answer: it is decisive for **0047**, and probably not decisive for **4585/4586**, for reasons
that are about places rather than money.

**Why it may not solve the clinical doctorate problem.** The School's "no full-time admission without
a scholarship" position is not documented university policy, so whether private funding satisfies it
is a discretionary call I cannot answer from the outside. But there is a structural reason to be
pessimistic. The School's own language is about being "considered for a **course and scholarship
offer**" as a single thing, which suggests cohort size is set by the number of funded places. If
that is right, the scarce good is the place, not the money, and self-funding does not create one.

On top of that, the binding constraint on these two programs is almost certainly clinical placement
and supervision capacity, not stipends. Each candidate needs a minimum of 1,000 supervised hours
across three placements in partner health services, under APAC-accredited supervision ratios. You
cannot buy a placement. And the MNHS Expression of Interest form requires the supervisor to declare
"sufficient resources, within my control, are available for the proposed project" — a resourcing test
that is separate from your living costs.

There is also a plain reason a school worried about duty of care might decline a family arrangement:
it is not enforceable and the University has no mechanism to verify or hold it. A promise from a
parent can be withdrawn; a scholarship cannot.

**So ask the right question.** Do not ask "will you accept my father's sponsorship". Ask:

> Is admission to 4585/4586 capped by the number of funded stipends, or by placement and supervision
> capacity? If a place and a supervisor are available, can an externally funded or self-funded
> candidate be admitted? If so, what form and evidence of financial support does the School require?

That single question separates two very different worlds and tells you whether this route exists at
all.

**Ask also whether it can be formalised.** The *RTP Scholarship Procedure* states in its scope that
it "does not apply to externally-funded scholarships" — so externally funded scholarships are a
recognised category at Monash. It is worth asking whether a privately funded scholarship can be
established and administered through the University rather than paid to you directly. That converts
an informal family promise into a documented award with a named funder and a term, which is a far
stronger answer to a duty-of-care objection than a bank statement.

**Where the sponsorship is genuinely powerful: 0047.** For a research PhD as a domestic student,
tuition is already $0 under the RTP Fees Offset, there is no placement bottleneck, and the
duty-of-care reasoning does not apply in the same way because there is no fixed timetable. $37,145/yr
of family support replicates precisely what the stipend would have provided. That removes funding as
a barrier entirely, and it changes the plan in `monash-phd-pathway.md` in one important way: you
would no longer need to *win* the competitive stipend to start. You would only need a supervisor to
say yes.

Concretely, that makes **domestic Round 2, closing 31 May 2027 for a July–December 2027 start**, a
realistic full-time commencement that depends on nothing you cannot control. Roughly seven months
earlier than February 2028, and no lottery.

Two footnotes. If you later win a stipend you can hold both — clause 4.28.3 permits receiving "a
concurrent stipend(s) to support the recipient's general living costs". And on tax: a genuine gift
from a family member is generally not assessable income in Australia, unlike a part-time stipend
which is taxable under 4.20, but get proper advice rather than relying on this.

## Residency status: do not apply as domestic before you hold PR

You asked whether you could apply as domestic now and have PR by the time payment falls due. Two
things about this.

**First, don't.** The MNHS graduate research application asks you to state citizenship as either
"Domestic (Australian/New Zealand Citizen or Australian Permanent Resident)" or "International", and
carries this declaration: "I warrant that the information on this form is correct and complete. I
acknowledge that the provision of incorrect information or the withholding of relevant information
relating to my application ... might invalidate my application and that the University may withdraw
an offer of a place or cancel my enrolment in consequence. Should the University determine that I
have submitted a false document, I consent to the University disclosing this information to other
relevant tertiary institutions."

Declaring domestic status you do not hold is not a technicality with a fee consequence. The stated
outcomes are an invalidated application, a withdrawn offer, cancelled enrolment, and disclosure to
other universities. Against a plan that spans several years and a professional registration that
depends on good character, that is an unacceptable risk for a few months' timing gain. The sector
norm is the same everywhere: you apply as international until PR is actually granted.

**Second, and more usefully: you don't need the workaround.** Work the dates.

- PR expected around **March 2027**.
- The February 2028 clinical doctorate deadlines will fall around **31 July 2027** (international) and
  **mid-September 2027** (domestic), based on the current cycle.

March 2027 comes comfortably before both. So by the time you apply for a February 2028 start you will
already hold PR and will be a genuine domestic applicant. There is nothing to finesse. The only
intake where the timing gap bites is February 2027, and that one is already closed on the
international side (deadline 31 July 2026) and needs PR you will not have by 15 September 2026.

**What the mechanism actually is, for completeness.** Fee status does move mid-course, and it is
well documented. *Graduate Research Enrolment Procedure* 2.10–2.11.1: "Changes to citizenship or
residency status may affect the fees charged for the applicable enrolment period," and "the date for
the change in residency status is the visa or citizenship grant date." You notify via the Change of
Residency Status form, and clause 2.12 of the *RTP Scholarship Procedure* then applies — all domestic
students receive the RTP Fees Offset. So the legitimate version of your idea does exist: apply
**truthfully as international**, and if PR is granted before or during candidature, convert and pick
up the Fees Offset from the grant date.

That is a genuine contingency plan if PR slips past September 2027 — apply in the international round
for February 2028 and convert on grant. The catch is that as an international applicant you need both
a stipend *and* a tuition scholarship, which is the harder combination. Also confirm what happens to
an awarded scholarship on conversion, since clause 2.6 bars holding a University tuition scholarship
alongside an equivalent Australian Government award.

**Verify:** whether fee status for graduate research admission is assessed at application or at
commencement, and whether Monash will consider an application on the basis of expected status at
commencement with evidence of a lodged PR application. I could not confirm either way, and it is
worth asking before 15 September 2026 in case February 2027 is salvageable.
