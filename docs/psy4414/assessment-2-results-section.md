## Why this section matters

The results section is the engine of the manuscript. It is where everything comes
together: you asked a research question, you stated hypotheses, and (depending on
your methodology) you set out an aim. The results section is where you actually
answer that question, using methods of research to respond to the original idea
you set out to test.

The section has to provide enough detail to stand as evidence for what you found,
and that evidence carries a level of credibility that rests on the rigour of your
analysis. Whether that is thematic analysis in a qualitative study or statistical
analysis in a quantitative one, the rigour of the work comes to the fore here.

### Why qualitative gets more words

Qualitative results use extracts from people, participants, or secondary data
sources to support the themes being constructed — those extracts *are* the
evidence, and they count towards the word limit. The quantitative equivalent is
tables and models, which do not count towards the word count. Hence the
difference in the two limits.

### Why an analysis output is required

Presenting what you found is not sufficient on its own. You need to show where
the information came from, because that is part of the education verification
process: it demonstrates that you understand where your data comes from.

### Participant flow

You must account for how participants entered the study and where the numbers
went. If you approached 1,000 people in a quantitative study and ended with 100,
or approached 10 people in a qualitative study and ended with five, explain the
difference. Typical explanations include people not committing to an interview
(qualitative), passive responding by several hundred participants, and
participants who did not complete all measures — each step whittling the sample
down to the final number. Your supervisor can help you construct this.

Two short checklists accompany the assessment slide and set out exactly what is
needed in each section; the participant flow sits alongside them.

## Quantitative results — what to include

### Data cleaning

Include all the required information. That covers participants whose responses
were not viable because they responded passively or raced through the assessment,
reverse scoring, and anything else that shaped the dataset. You need a strong
understanding of what happened to your data and to be able to account for it.

### Assumption testing

Include all assumption testing for your analysis. This cannot be skipped: missing
assumption testing throws your entire results into jeopardy and undermines their
credibility.

### Descriptive statistics

Descriptives are required so the reader understands exactly what is happening in
the data. If you are running mediation or moderation, report the means and
establish the intercorrelational relationships between the variables in your
study. Remember you are recounting what you did for someone else's understanding,
so make the most of the descriptives to let the marker see where your data comes
from.

Note the scope, per the FAQ: this subsection describes your **study variables**,
not your sample. Demographics go in the Method unless they bear directly on
hypothesis testing.

### Inferential statistics

These can be presented as a paragraph, with diagrams, or in whatever form your
analysis needs. Follow the conventions you learned and used in PSY4401 — those
conventions are sound and are held in high regard in manuscript marking. APA 7th
also requires **effect sizes and confidence intervals**, and non-significant
results are reported just as fully as significant ones.

## Qualitative results — what to include

- **Introduce your themes at the start** of the results so the reader knows what
  is coming.
- **Use a theme map.** It conveys what the themes are doing, how they relate, and
  what happens where themes are connected. It sets the results section up to be
  understood.
- **Integrate themes and extracts in accordance with the guidance** attached to
  the assessment. That guidance was written by experts and reflects an
  industry-ready standard for placing extracts into a paper.
- **Apply the analytic lens to the data.** Your interpretation of what
  participants or the data are saying — across participants, between
  participants, and within participants — and the themes that emerge from it are
  the quintessential essence of your research results. This matters greatly when
  the manuscripts are assessed at the end.
- **Write a wrap-up.** Do not leave the section open-ended; summarise what is
  happening so the reader is led into the discussion section that follows.

## Helpful hints

1. **Understand what the assessment is actually asking you to do.**
2. **Reuse what you already have.** You wrote a results section back in PSY4401 —
   use those resources. If you are doing qualitative work, use the workbooks on
   the Moodle site; they are there to scaffold you through.
3. **Refer back to your data analysis plan (DAP) from PSY4413.** If you are only
   starting your analysis now, that is a dangerous position to be in when
   submission is at the end of the week.
4. **Get an early start.** Begin writing content and have your aims and a draft of
   your results ready, ideally before the week 1 supervision session.
5. **Ask questions in supervision.** You have two supervision opportunities before
   submission — do not miss them, and use them wisely.
6. **Act on your supervisor's feedback.** What they tell you is what should go
   into your manuscript, because that is what counts towards your final grade.
7. **Read the sample theses.** Even where formatting differs, the results sections
   are very worthwhile and are close to on point for this assessment. Some may
   run longer in places than what you will submit.
