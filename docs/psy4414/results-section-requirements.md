Consolidated from four unit documents, which override the walkthrough video notes
wherever they differ:

- **Rubric** — *PSY4414 A2 Journal Manuscript: Results Marking Rubric*
- **Guidelines** — *GDPA Journal Manuscript Submission Guidelines (Quantitative), v4*
- **FAQ** — *PSY4414 Results Section FAQ* (last modified 5 May 2026)
- **Workbook** — *Results Section Workbook, 1. Getting Started*

## The four required subsections

The Guidelines are explicit: a quantitative Results section has these subsections,
in this order.

1. **Data Cleaning**
2. **Assumption Testing**
3. **Preliminary Analyses (Descriptive Statistics)**
4. **Inferential Statistics**

`Results` is an APA Level 1 heading; the subsections use lower APA heading levels.
The Results section begins immediately after the Method — **not** on a new page.

## The first page, before the Results proper

Easy to miss and specifically required by the FAQ: before the Results section
itself, include your **aim**, your **hypotheses / research questions** (from the
Introduction) and your **design** (from the Method).

This material is *not graded*. But if there is a mismatch between your research
question or hypothesis, your design, and the analysis you actually conducted, that
mismatch **can** be considered in the grading. The FAQ advises confirming the
wording of your hypotheses with your supervisor so they map cleanly onto your
analysis — particularly if anything has shifted since PSY4412 and PSY4413.

## What goes where — the placement rules

This is where most marks are quietly lost, because several statistics that feel
like "results" belong in the Method.

| Statistic | Where it goes | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cronbach's alpha for **previously validated** scales | **Method**, Measures subsection — it is a baseline descriptive of the scale | FAQ |
| Cronbach's alpha where the study **develops or refines** a scale | **Results**, most likely Inferential Statistics | FAQ |
| Descriptive statistics of **study variables** | **Results**, Preliminary Analyses | FAQ |
| Descriptive statistics of the **sample** (demographics) | **Method**, unless directly relevant to hypothesis testing | FAQ |
| Correlations that **are** part of hypothesis testing | **Results**, Inferential Statistics | FAQ |
| Correlations that are **preliminary** to a hypothesised regression model | **Results**, Preliminary Analyses | FAQ |
| Scale scoring detail already given in the Method | Do not repeat, unless the reader needs it to understand how a variable was formed (e.g. a continuous scale recoded as categorical) | FAQ |
| Assumption testing | **Results**, before the inferential analyses; in text or supported by tables | FAQ |

## Reporting rules

- **Do not interpret in the Results.** Present the statistical findings
  objectively. Interpretation and implications belong in the Discussion. Note the
  distinction from the rubric's "Results: Interpretation" criterion, which is about
  *correct statistical* interpretation of your cleaning, assumptions, descriptives
  and inferentials — not substantive or theoretical interpretation of what the
  findings mean.
- **Report effect sizes and confidence intervals.** Required, consistent with APA
  7th, because they convey magnitude and precision.
- **Report non-significant results objectively.** Every analysis conducted to test
  the hypotheses is presented, regardless of significance.
- **Include post hoc analyses** where relevant and clearly justified.
- **Past tense** throughout the Results.
- **Australian English** spelling.
- Additional analyses beyond your Data Analysis Plan are acceptable if minor,
  justified, discussed with your supervisor, and aligned to your aims.

## Tables and figures

- They do **not** count towards the word count.
- They must follow **APA 7th** formatting and be **referenced in the text**.
- Tables and figures cited in the manuscript appear in the body, **not** in an
  appendix. Supplementary tables and figures may go in the Appendix, but they must
  support rather than replace what is in the Results.

## Word count

The Guidelines give the final Journal Manuscript a total of 7,000–8,000 words, with
**Results 1,500–2,000 words** as a general guideline. Note there is no 10% leeway on
the overall total and reviewers stop reading at 8,000 words.

The A2 milestone limit stated in the walkthrough video was up to ~1,200 words for
quantitative and up to 2,000 for qualitative, but the audio was unclear and none of
these four documents states the milestone limit. **Confirm the A2 figure against
the assessment page on Moodle.**

## The output file

Both the Guidelines and the FAQ treat this as a separate, required submission.

- Named `Surname-GDPA-output`, submitted alongside `Surname-GDPA-journal manuscript`.
- No word limit and no APA formatting requirement, but order it to match the order
  of your Results section, and label it so a reviewer can follow along.
- Include **only** what is relevant to the final version of your Results section.
  Trim anything not reported.

For a quantitative project it should contain:

- the final reliability analysis for scales and subscales;
- data cleaning relevant to your decision-making (missing value analysis, for example);
- descriptive analysis **of the variables, not the sample**;
- assumption testing;
- inferential analysis for **each** hypothesised model;
- **the syntax boxes produced with the output.**

That last point matters: the FAQ says syntax should be included where relevant
*because it lets the marker verify the analyses you conducted*. Custom syntax is
not required if you did not use any. The Guidelines assume SPSS output, and point
to *Supplement 1 — SPSS Output* for further detail.

Saving SPSS output: **File → Export → Word/RTF (\*.doc)**, or export directly to
PDF. On vLAB, output may be saving to your Monash drive, so map that drive locally
if you cannot find the file.

## Problems you may hit, and what the unit says to do

**A violated assumption.** The FAQ is reassuring here: real-world data often does
not conform, and there is rarely a need to panic. Look at the data from multiple
perspectives — the statistical tests, the visual checks, and your own understanding
of the variables. Discuss it with your supervisor *before* making dramatic changes,
especially transformations that make findings hard to interpret. Sometimes a
non-parametric alternative is better; sometimes you proceed as planned and
acknowledge the impact later in the manuscript.

**Reliability below .70.** Contact your supervisor *before* running your analysis,
not after.

**Referencing software.** APA does not require references for commonly used programs
such as SPSS. Less common ones — the Hayes PROCESS macro, for instance — do need to
be referenced.

## What the rubric rewards

Six criteria are applied, each graded HD through Fail.

| Criterion | What separates a High Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| **Knowledge and Alignment** | Responds directly and comprehensively to the research questions, aims and hypotheses; findings reported with exceptional clarity and accuracy using the appropriate analytical approach; results presented logically and systematically with **all** outcomes explicitly linked to the stated objectives; outputs fully support the manuscript's argument |
| **Reporting** | Succinct, coherent reporting with outstanding presentation of data, including sophisticated and appropriate use of visual elements; statistical outputs reported **and interpreted correctly** |
| **Rational judgement** | Accurate rational judgement and appropriate **justification of the data analysis process** — data cleaning, assumption testing, descriptive analyses, inferential analyses |
| **Interpretation** | Accurate statistical and logical interpretation across cleaning, assumptions, descriptives and inferentials |
| **Clarity of style** | Excellent structure and logical layout using headings and subheadings; very clear, succinct, scholarly prose; correct grammar, spelling and syntax |
| **Citations and Referencing** | Correct in-text citation and referencing in APA 7th |

Two patterns worth reading off this. First, three of the six criteria reward
**justifying and correctly interpreting the analysis process itself**, not just
reporting numbers — so explain *why* you cleaned as you did and *what* each
assumption test showed. Second, "all outcomes explicitly linked to the stated
objectives" is the HD descriptor under Knowledge and Alignment, which is the
mechanical reason the aim and hypotheses go on that first page.

## Formatting

- A4, double spaced throughout.
- Margins: 1 inch top, bottom and right; 1.5 inches on the left.
- Times New Roman 12pt, Arial 11pt, or Calibri 11pt.
- Running head in all capitals, 50 characters or fewer, left-aligned in the header
  opposite the right-aligned page number, on every page including the first. Do not
  write the label "Running head:".
- Consecutive page numbers, top right.

## Data storage

A copy of the data must be kept for **five years**, per NHMRC requirements under
your MUHREC approval. Liaise with your supervisor about where it is stored. This is
also why nothing in this repository's `data/` directory is ever committed — see
[`data/README.md`](../../data/README.md).
