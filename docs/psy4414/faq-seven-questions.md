# Seven answers to seven commonly asked questions

These are the closing points of the assessment walkthrough, covering the questions
students ask most often about the results section.

## 1. I haven't started my results section yet

Start as soon as you finish watching the video. Starting early matters because you
may run into problems, and there may need to be changes made to support you
through them — which takes time you will not have if you begin late.

The broader point: the programme is meant to produce independent researchers. You
should be driving the conversations in supervision and driving your own research
effort. That is the skill set APAC expects graduates to leave with. Master's
programmes are intense, fast-paced, and demand a great deal of your time in
research as well as in practitioner work. The pace differs substantially between
undergraduate, postgraduate, honours-level, and master's programmes, so build
those working habits now.

## 2. What should the write-up be based on?

Write your results section based on the feedback you received on your data
analysis plan — not just the DAP you submitted, but the feedback on it. Make sure
you actually understand that feedback.

## 3. How do I check I'm on the right track?

Look at your method and triangulate it against your supervisor's feedback. Your
supervisor knows you and knows your project best, and you have two opportunities
with them before you submit. Come to those sessions knowing what you want to talk
about so you can produce the best results section possible.

## 4. I'm unsure what to do

Work through the available support:

- Rewatch the walkthrough video.
- Check the Moodle resources.
- Check the FAQs.
- Work through the Moodle workbooks.
- Ask on the Q&A forum, where support is also provided.

## 5. What should I take from the sample theses?

Use them, and take note of what is required and where things are located within
the section.

## 6. Does the structure change between quantitative and qualitative?

Not much. The results section is surprisingly similar across all styles of
quantitative and qualitative work, and there is little deviation. A checklist is
provided that tells you how things need to be laid out, so following it is close
to paint-by-numbers: put the required information in the right place, write it up
in line with PSY4401 conventions, and the results section is essentially done.

## 7. What is most important in a qualitative thesis?

Applying the lens to the data analysis. Your interpretation of what participants
or the data say — across, between, and within participants — and the themes that
emerge from it are the essence of your research results. Spend the time on it.

## And finally

Your supervisor knows your project best. They started the ideation with you, gave
you the project, followed it through, and gave you feedback. They are well versed
and published in the area. Consult them; they can give you the support you need.
