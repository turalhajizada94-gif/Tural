## Assessment 2 — Results section

Let's now turn our attention to assessment two, the results section — probably one
of the most important sections in your manuscript. The results section is the
engine for your manuscript. It is the place where everything comes together.
You've asked a research question. You've asked a series of hypotheses. You may
have provided an aim, depending on which type of study and methodology you
perform. And now you're actually answering that question. So you're using methods
of research to be able to respond to the original idea of you trying to ask this
question in research, or this hypothesis you want to test in research.

And so now we're really saying, how do things come together in this part of the
manuscript that then provide enough detail to provide evidence for what you have
found? And that evidence includes a level of credibility in the analysis you do.
So the rigour with which you undertake either thematic analysis using qual studies,
or quant studies being statistical analyses, really does come to the fore in this
particular section of the manuscript.

### Word limits

The results section will have different word length counts based on the type of
methodology. Quantitative studies up to [1,]200 words, qualitative studies up to
2,000 words. The reason the qualitative studies have more is because they use
extracts. They use people, participants, or secondary data sources to support the
themes that they're creating. That is their evidence. It's the same as the table
or the models that you would put into a quantitative project — they don't count to
your word count, whereas these extracts do in qualitative. So that's the reason
why there is a slight difference in the word count length.

### Hurdle, outputs and participant flow

This is a competency hurdle. You must get through. You will receive feedback for
this. And all projects need to submit an output showing your analysis. It's not
sufficient just to present what you've found. You need to show us where that
information came from, because that's part of the education verification process
— that we understand that you understand where the data is coming from. There's
two short checklists on the side of the slide here which tell you exactly what you
need in each section.

Bear in mind you will also need your participant flow, which is the accounting of
the way participants came in. For example, in either project, if you approached 10
people in qualitative or a thousand people in quantitative, and you only ended up
with 100 in quantitative and five in your qualitative, you need to account for the
differences. So you need to say people didn't commit to coming to an interview for
qualitative, or passive responses were given by 700 participants and then another
few participants didn't complete all measures, and it dwindles down to the final
number. You need to justify your participant flow, and your supervisor can help
you with that.

### Quantitative — data cleaning and analysis

Let's go through the sections of the quant data cleaning and parts of the results
section in a little bit of detail to give you an understanding.

Data cleaning is vitally important. Make sure you put all the required information
in there, including things like if you had people who passively, or really really
quickly, ran through the assessment, making those responses not viable. You need
to account for how you data clean — reverse scoring, etc. You need to make sure
that you've got a really good understanding about what's happening with your data.

Assumption testing: make sure you include all assumption testing for your
analysis. You cannot miss these assumptions. If you do, it throws into jeopardy
your entire results and undermines the credibility of your results. So please make
sure that you include your assumption testing.

Descriptive statistics are required. You need to provide us with the descriptives
so we understand exactly what's happening. If you're doing mediation or
moderation, you need to tell us what the means are. You need to tell us that
there's an intercorrelational relationship between the variables in your particular
study. So make the most of the descriptives to provide the markers the ability to
be able to see where your data is coming from and understand it, because you're
now recounting what you did for somebody else's understanding, and it's really
important you understand how to do that well.

Inferentials can be as a paragraph, with diagrams, whatever it is that you need.
Follow the convention of PSY4401 and the work that you did there. Those
conventions are sound and we hold them in high regard in our manuscript marking.
So please use the resources that you already have used in the past and write up
your associated statistics based on what you've seen.

### Qualitative — themes, extracts and the lens

In qualitative, please introduce your themes at the start of your results to tell
us what's happening. Theme maps are really, really good. Let us know exactly what's
happening so that we get this great sensibility about what you're actually going
to talk about in your results section. Again, I mentioned the theme map on the
other side. Tell us what these themes are doing, how they relate, what happens
when you have a relationship between the themes. It really does position the
results section up for understanding.

Include the themes and extracts in accordance with the guidance. We actually have
guidance which is attached to the assessment on how to integrate extracts into
your manuscript results. So please follow that best guidance. It's been provided
by experts to provide you with industry-ready level of preparedness to be able to
put extracts into a paper.

And make sure you have a wrap-up at the end. Tell us what's actually happening.
Please don't just leave it open-ended. Try and provide at least some summary of
what's actually happening to lead you into the discussion section, which is after
the results section of course.
