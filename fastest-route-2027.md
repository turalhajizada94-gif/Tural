# Fastest route to clinical psychologist / neuropsychologist + doctorate

Verified 26 August 2026. Situation: not yet PR, PR possibly granted by February 2027, GDPA complete
2 October 2026, WAM 70–75, savings available, three years disability support work.
Goal: registered and endorsed as fast as possible, with a doctorate, ideally with fees covered and a
stipend.

## Verification of the prior analysis

### Confirmed

**The registrar hours ladder and the 75% dual-endorsement discount.** Both are in the Board's
guidelines verbatim: "A psychologist who is not endorsed but is simultaneously seeking two
endorsements ... is required to undertake 75 per cent of the supervision required for each
endorsement. For example, a psychologist who would normally be required to undertake 3,000 hours ...
following completion of a dual professional Doctoral Degree (1,500 hours for each area of practice),
must undertake 1,125 hours in each area (that is, a total of 2,250 hours)."

**Early general registration.** "Early general registration is available for Doctoral Degree
candidates who have completed all the coursework and placements for their degree and have made
substantial progress with their Doctoral thesis."

One qualifier the earlier analysis missed: you can start the registrar program early, but you cannot
be *endorsed* until the thesis is marked — "Applicants must have completed all components of the
relevant postgraduate qualification (including having received the final mark for any thesis) prior to
applying for endorsement."

**Supervisor scarcity.** "Principal supervisors of registrars in an area of practice must have held
endorsement in that area of practice for at least two years."

**Experience earns nothing toward endorsement.** Correct. Your support work is an admission asset only.

**Swinburne's scholarship round.** Fully confirmed, and better than described. Swinburne Research
Stipend Scholarships: Round 2 opens 7 September 2026, **closes Friday 9 October 2026, 11.59pm**. Open
to "Local and international students". Eligible courses: "Doctor of Philosophy (PhD), **Professional
Doctorate**". Value: **$35,500/yr stipend (2026 rate) for three years** plus "a RTP Fees-Offset
(Domestic) Scholarship (domestic students) **or a Tuition Fee Scholarship (international students)
which covers tuition fees for up to four years**", plus relocation and thesis allowances. Supporting
documents due 16 October 2026. International Round 2 recipients must enrol by 29 June 2027.

**One intake a year, no mid-year entry.** Confirmed across the sector. Swinburne DPsych: "Semester 1
2027 – closing 9 October 2026, 11:59pm. Applications submitted after this date will not be accepted."
Melbourne: January, 15 September deadline. Deakin: Trimester 1 (March). Monash: February only.

**The conversion split.** Confirmed. For a research degree, domestic status brings the RTP Fees Offset
automatically — RMIT states it plainly: "If you are an Australian citizen, Australian permanent
resident or New Zealand citizen your place is automatically funded by the Research Training Program
(RTP) by an RTP Fee Offset scholarship." For coursework it is discretionary: UNSW warns that "changing
your residency status does not guarantee a Commonwealth Supported Place ... places are limited for
postgraduate programs and are based on merit", and that you "remain as a domestic fee-paying student
until you have applied for a CSP".

**A new risk on the coursework side.** UNSW also states: "If your residency status changes **before you
accept your offer**, your existing offer as an international fee-paying student will be **withdrawn**."
So for a coursework master's, PR landing between offer and acceptance can void the offer and push you
to the next intake. That is a specific reason not to route a coursework application through a
conversion.

### Corrected

**Deakin's Doctor of Psychology (Clinical) is NOT domestic-only.** This was wrong, and it matters
because Deakin is otherwise the strongest option on the list. Deakin publishes a dedicated
international course page for it, it carries CRICOS code 022556D, and international entry requirements
are published including the APS equivalence requirement. Verify the international application route
and closing date directly, but do not exclude it.

**The bridging figure.** A bridging program to a second endorsement is 3,000 hours over 88 weeks as a
sixth-year qualification; the 2,250 figure comes from the separate 75% multiple-endorsement discount.
The earlier correction was right about this.

### Newly resolved — the accreditation year level

This was flagged as the open question that decides registrar hours. It is now answered from APAC's own
accreditation reports.

**Deakin Doctor of Psychology (Clinical): accredited "Level 3-4, 5–7 year, Accreditation without
Conditions."** Seventh year. So it attracts the shortest registrar tier: **1,500 hours over 44 weeks.**
For comparison, the same APAC table lists Deakin's Master of Psychology (Organisational) as "Level 3-4,
5-6 year" — the distinction is recorded per program, and Deakin's clinical doctorate is on the right
side of it.

**Swinburne Doctor of Psychology (Clinical and Forensic): almost certainly seventh year too.** APAC
lists it as "packaged Level 3 and 4 Clinical, and combined Clinical and Forensic psychology", and
APAC's evidence guide specifies that a "Doctoral Degree (Professional) ... for Level 3 and 4 graduate
competencies, that is, a PsyBA-approved 5th, 6th and 7th year of study ... include at least 1500
placement hours". Swinburne's course requires "at least 1500 hours of placement", which matches the
doctoral profile rather than the 1,000-hour master's profile. Confirm in writing, but the evidence
points to seventh year.

Course length plus minimum registrar duration. Registrar time is paid employment, not study, and can

Two conclusions fall out:

1. **The fastest route to endorsement alone is a two-year master's** — roughly 3.7 years. But it gives
   no doctorate and the longest registrar program.
2. **The fastest route to endorsement *and* a doctorate is Deakin's DPsych (Clinical)** — roughly
   4.5–5 years, because seventh-year accreditation cuts the registrar program to 1,500 hours. It beats
   Monash's clinical doctorates by about 750 registrar hours and beats master's-then-PhD by roughly two
   years.

**The neuropsychology caveat.** Deakin and Swinburne are clinical (and forensic), not neuropsychology.
In Melbourne, neuropsychology means Melbourne 527CN (master's, 3,000 registrar hours), La Trobe
(requires H1/80% in fourth year — out of reach), or Monash 4586 (closed for 2027 and stipend-gated).
If you insist on neuropsychology, every route is slower and harder. Decide this deliberately.

## The answer to your funding question

**"Where can I apply as an international now to get no fees plus a stipend?"** Swinburne, and it is the
only one identified. A professional doctorate is an eligible course for the Swinburne Research Stipend,
the round is open to international students, and it bundles a Tuition Fee Scholarship with a $35,500
stipend. Both the course application and the scholarship application close 9 October 2026.

**The honest caveat.** Scholarship selection favours First Class Honours equivalent, and you are H2A.
Course entry is H2A, so admission and scholarship are separate decisions — you may well be admitted
without the scholarship. Selection also weighs referee reports, research experience relative to
opportunity, and relevant work experience, and the course page notes counselling experience is
preferable, which your support work speaks to. Treat the scholarship as a real but unlikely bonus.

**"If I can't get the scholarship, can I just enrol and pay my own living costs?"** Yes — and this is
where the research-degree structure saves you. If admitted as an international without a scholarship
you face $43,000/yr. But it is a research degree, so the moment PR is granted you become an RTP
student and tuition drops to $0 automatically, with nothing discretionary to miss. Your exposure is
limited to the teaching periods before PR lands. With a March 2027 start and PR in February 2027, that
exposure is potentially zero.

Note the timing rule: if residency is granted after a census date, the change takes effect from the
next census date. So a March start with PR in February clears comfortably. Melbourne's January intake
is tighter.

**"Should I wait for 2028 instead?"** No. Applying now costs one application fee and preserves the
option. The downside case is bounded (a quarter or two of international fees, only if PR is late and
you choose to commence anyway), and you can decline an offer. Waiting for 2028 costs a guaranteed year.
