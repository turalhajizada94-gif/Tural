# Getting into Monash 4586 (Clinical Neuropsychology) for February 2028

Execution plan. Companion to `monash-phd-pathway.md` and `monash-course-options.md`.
Written 25 August 2026. Target: full-time commencement February 2028, self-funded, domestic.

## The honest answer up front

**You cannot almost-guarantee 4586.** Savings solve the funding problem completely — as a domestic
student your tuition is $0 under the RTP Fees Offset, so with living costs covered you are financially
sorted. But funding was never the hard part. 4586 is a *ranked competitive selection* into a small
APAC-accredited clinical cohort whose size is limited by clinical placement and supervision capacity.
Money cannot create a placement.

It is also, as far as I can establish, **the only direct-entry doctoral clinical neuropsychology
program in Australia that accepts H2A**. The other doctoral route, Melbourne's 080CN, is explicitly
not direct entry — "Prospective students must be enrolled in the first year of Master of Psychology
(Clinical Neuropsychology) to be considered" — and its PhD component requires a weighted average of
80%. Everything else in the country (UQ, UNSW, Macquarie, UWA) is a master's, not a doctorate.

So the target set for what you actually want is effectively **one program, one intake per year**. That
is why "almost guarantee" is not available, and any advice that says otherwise is selling you
something.

What *is* available: maximising your rank in a selection system Monash publishes, and building a
fallback ladder so that February 2028 produces a full-time doctorate even if 4586 says no.

## The genuinely good news

Monash publishes how it selects for these programs, and it is much more favourable to you than the
"WAM 80+" framing suggests.

> "Invitation for an interview is based on overall suitability ... which takes into account a
> combination of **academic marks, referee reports and video statement**."
>
> "Interviewed applicants will be **ranked for offer based on a system that combines academic marks,
> referee reports, video statement, and interview**."
>
> "**Grades are important but not the sole criterion.** Relevant volunteer or work experience, clear
> communication skills, and a genuine interest in [the] field are important. Personal statements
> [and] referee reports also play a significant role."

Read that carefully. The offer ranking has **four** components and your WAM is one of them. Three of
the four — referee reports, video statement, interview — are things you can deliberately build over
the next twelve months. Monash has also stated in writing that grades are not the sole criterion and
that volunteer or work experience matters.

This is a fundamentally different game from the central scholarship round, where a WAM ranking
dominates. It is the reason a 70–75 applicant can be competitive here, and it is where all your
effort should go.

## Resolve this before anything else

The School told you it does not admit full-time candidates without a scholarship, on duty-of-care
grounds. **If that is a hard rule, self-funding does not help you at all** and the entire plan has to
be about winning a stipend instead. If it is a financial-security concern that evidence of savings
can satisfy, your position is much stronger than a scholarship applicant's, because you are asking
for a place rather than a place plus money.

These two worlds require opposite strategies, so settle it first. Email
`psych-gradresearch-enquiries@monash.edu` and ask, in these terms:

> As a domestic applicant my tuition would be covered by the RTP Fees Offset. If I can evidence
> personal savings sufficient to cover living costs for the full duration, can I be admitted to 4586
> without an RTP stipend?
>
> Relatedly: is the size of the 4586 cohort determined by the number of funded stipends, or by
> clinical placement and supervision capacity?
>
> And: are 4586 applicants ranked for stipends within the clinical cohort, or against the general
> graduate research applicant pool?

The second question is the one that tells you whether self-funding is even conceptually possible. The
third tells you whether the WAM 80+ line applies to you at all.

## The twelve-month plan

Ordered by how much each item moves your rank.

### 1. The GDPA research project mark — next six weeks

Your last chance to move the academic component, and it is weighted in both the interview invitation
and the final ranking. The research project units are also the part of your transcript a
neuropsychology selection panel will look at hardest. Everything else on this list takes months;
this takes six weeks and then it is fixed forever.

### 2. Neuropsychology-specific clinical experience — from October 2026

Monash names "relevant volunteer or work experience" explicitly. For neuropsychology specifically,
target settings that match 4586's placement domains — "child and adolescent, acute neurology,
geriatric, psychiatric and rehabilitation":

- Brain injury and stroke rehabilitation services
- Memory clinics and dementia services (Dementia Australia and similar)
- Aged care and geriatric assessment
- Disability services and NDIS-funded support work
- Neurology or rehabilitation ward volunteering
- Crisis lines and mental health support roles

Twelve months of consistent, documented work in one or two of these is worth far more than a
scattering of short placements, because it gives a referee something specific to write about and
gives you concrete material for the video statement and interview. If you can get *paid* work in one
of these settings, better — it doubles as income and evidence.

### 3. Referee reports — cultivate from now, not from August 2027

Three are required. The ideal set covers research capability, clinical aptitude and reliability under
supervision. That means you need people who have actually watched you work for months. Candidates
lose here by asking near-strangers late. Identify your three by December 2026 and make sure each has
supervised you on something substantial by mid-2027. Your GDPA project supervisor should be one.

### 4. Research assistant work in a neuropsychology group — from October 2026

This does several things at once: builds the research experience that Admissions clause 1.10.3
recognises, gets you co-authorship, produces a referee who is a known quantity to the School, and
makes you a familiar face rather than an application. Target Monash's Turner Institute and the
Monash Institute of Cognitive and Clinical Neurosciences, which delivers 4586. Prof Antonio
Verdejo-García's Addiction and Impulsivity Research lab is the closest match to your stated interests
and is still at Monash. BrainPark under Prof Manos Stamatakis is another.

Being known to the people who sit on the selection panel is not a trick, it is how small clinical
cohorts actually get filled.

### 5. A research output — from your GDPA thesis

A conference abstract or a submitted paper. Under Admissions clause 1.10.2 this counts as a research
output, and for a 4586 application where 70% of the degree is a 60,000–80,000 word thesis, evidence
that you can carry a project to publication is directly on point. Talk to your GDPA supervisor about
this before you finish, while the data is fresh and they are still invested.

### 6. Provisional registration with AHPRA — not yet available

Correction: you cannot hold provisional registration while unenrolled. The PsyBA standard requires
that you "need provisional registration in order to complete one of the following programs of
supervised practice", and the application requires you to nominate the fifth-year program and
provider you will train at, with the provider supplying evidence of enrolment.

This costs you nothing, because the entry requirements for these programs say *eligible for*
provisional registration rather than *holding* it, and your completed GDPA makes you eligible. You
will apply once you commence in February 2028. See `perth-to-melbourne-roadmap.md`.

### 7. The video statement — draft from early 2027, film in August 2027

Three minutes, on "your vision for your career as a ... clinical neuropsychologist". This is the most
underprepared component in most applications and the most improvable. What separates a strong one:
it describes work you have actually done rather than intentions, it names a specific clinical
population and research question, and it explains why *neuropsychology* rather than clinical
psychology. Your interest in app-based cognitive assessment for addiction and compulsivity is an
unusually coherent answer to that last question — cognitive assessment is neuropsychology's core
business. Say that, concretely.

Write it in early 2027, revise it repeatedly, and record it only when the content is settled. Get
someone in the field to critique it.

### 8. Interview preparation — from July 2027

Interviews were 2–13 November in the current cycle. Expect similar. Prepare for questions on
clinical reasoning, ethical scenarios, why neuropsychology, your research interests, and how you
handle distress and supervision. Do mock interviews with someone who has been through a clinical
selection process.

### 9. Decide 4585 vs 4586 and commit

The video statement forces you to name one. A hedged answer reads as a hedged answer. Given your
research interests, 4586 is the coherent choice — but see the trade-offs in
`monash-course-options.md`, particularly the Medicare consequence if you ever want private therapy
practice.

## The fallback ladder

This is how you make February 2028 produce a full-time doctorate with high probability, even though
4586 itself cannot be guaranteed.

**Tier 1 — Monash 4586.** Primary target. Domestic round closing around mid-September 2027, results
required around mid-November 2027, interviews November 2027, outcomes early December 2027.

**Tier 2 — Monash 0047, self-funded, with a neuropsychology research project.** This is your floor,
and with savings behind you it is close to a certainty, because admission turns on a supervisor
agreeing and resources being available rather than on a competitive ranking. Tuition is $0. You get
the doctorate and the research career, but not the clinical licence.

Critically, the timing works: **admission-only applications can be lodged at any time of year** and
are "typically processed within six weeks". So a 4586 rejection in early December 2027 still leaves
you time to lodge a 0047 admission-only application and commence in the first half of 2028. You do
not lose the year.

Note the Monash constraint — one application per scholarship round or admission period — so these
must be sequential, not simultaneous.

**Tier 3 — other neuropsychology programs, if the clinical licence matters more than the doctorate.**
None of these is a direct-entry doctorate, but they all lead to neuropsychology endorsement:

| Program | Award | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UQ 5766 Master of Clinical Neuropsychology **and** Clinical Psychology | Master's, 2.5 yrs | Entry at honours class IIA, which you meet. Gives endorsement in **both** areas — the only program here that does |
| Melbourne 527CN Master of Psychology (Clinical Neuropsychology) | Master's | Gateway to 080CN combined PhD, but transfer needs an 80% average — confirm whether master's performance can substitute |
| UNSW, Macquarie, UWA | Master's | Additional shots at neuropsychology endorsement |

The UQ program is worth serious attention. It answers the question you asked earlier — it produces
endorsement in clinical neuropsychology *and* clinical psychology — and its entry bar is class IIA
honours, which your WAM meets. It is a master's rather than a doctorate, so you would need a PhD
afterwards for a research career, but as a route to being a dual-endorsed practitioner it is more
accessible than anything at Monash.

**Tier 4 — reapply to 4586 for February 2029** with a further year of experience. Not a failure
state; reapplication with a materially stronger file is common in clinical programs.

## What to accept

Two things, stated plainly.

First, you can make yourself a strong applicant, and with a 70–75 WAM against a four-component
ranking where three components are within your control, "strong" is genuinely achievable. But the
cohort is small and the decision is not yours. Plan for it to work and prepare for it not to.

Second, the fallback is not a consolation prize. A self-funded full-time 0047 with a good
neuropsychology supervisor gets you the doctorate, the research training, and the publication record
— which is what actually matters for the research career you described wanting in the first place.
What it does not get you is the clinical licence. Be honest with yourself about how much you want
that, because it is the difference between a plan that hinges on one committee's decision in December
2027 and a plan that does not.
